nothing touches disk on either side. raw audio is kept for exactly two frames (64 ms), as long as pitch needs, and then overwritten. what survives is features (a few numbers per 32 ms) and events. nothing is recorded or sent anywhere from this page.

what the cues are

reads ascuewhy
startle≥ startle_db over the floor, climbed ≥ startle_rise_db within 64 ms, loud outright, and still noise-like on the next frame (a clap, not a voice) the mammal startle reflex is a brainstem shortcut (≈ 6–12 ms in cats), decided before any listening. it fires on the frame, not after the sound.
onsetrises ≥ onset_db (soundsentry's 6×) out of the roomthe cue to turn toward a sound. the duck has one mic, so turning is done by sweeping the head and following the loudness.
calmnear your baseline in pitch and loudnessyour neutral voice, learned from the first 1.5 s you speak, then followed slowly from calm speech only.
cutesypitch ≥ cute_pitch_st over baseline, swinging sing-song, not shouting pet- and infant-directed speech raise mean pitch and widen its range; pet-directed matches infant-directed in pitch (burnham et al. 2002).
angry≥ angry_loud_db over your loudness, ≥ angry_min_dbfs outright, a pressed voice (richness: brightness ÷ pitch) hot anger is louder and pushes energy above 1 kHz (banse & scherer 1996). cute speech can be loud too, so a big sing-song pitch rise vetoes angry, not the other way round.
quiet / loudloudness ± loud_db / quiet_db from your baselinerelative, so it works at any distance from the mic once the baseline is set.

petting mutes everything (and ~1 s after): on the duck the mic is almost a contact mic, and a scratch is the loudest thing it hears.
